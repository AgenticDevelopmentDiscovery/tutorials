# Tutorials — Student-Led Peer Teaching

The **Presentations** assessment component (D-008, D-014). Each student (or pair) picks a topic
from the menu below, prepares a ~15-minute tutorial, and presents it to the cohort on a chosen
date. Tutorials are graded as participation and distributed across the term — they let the class
cover more ground than lectures alone, and they surface the fast-moving tooling no syllabus can
keep current.

## How it works
1. **Claim a topic + date** in the roster below (first come, first served; no two presenters on the
   same topic).
2. **Write it up** — expand the topic's brief into a tutorial **markdown file** and **share it with
   the cohort ahead of time**, so peers read it and arrive with questions.
3. **Present** ~15 minutes, ideally with a live demo.
4. **Field open Q&A** — be ready for questions that go **beyond** your prepared talk. Conceptual
   grasp, not delivery polish, is what's assessed.

You're graded on the [Tutorial / Presentation rubric](../assessment/rubric-tutorials.md): conceptual
mastery and honest question-handling first, then the written artifact, teaching, and course
connection (D-023).

## What a good tutorial does
Each brief in this folder follows the same shape, and so should your talk:
- **What it is** — the concept, clearly, for a cross-disciplinary audience.
- **Why it matters for agentic development** — the relevance, not just the definition.
- **Key ideas** — the 5–8 points that are the spine of the talk.
- **Hands-on / demo** — show it working, ideally on the course substrate (model discovery).
- **Connections** — tie it back to the Challenges, the capstone, or the two spines.
